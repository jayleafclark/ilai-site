Learning how to write a blog post that ranks and reads well comes down to three things: answer the reader's question in the first two sentences, structure the piece so a scanner and a reader both get value, and back every claim with a number or a named example. Search engines reward clarity and depth at the same time, not one over the other. This piece walks through the sequence we use at Ilai Collective when we draft long-form articles for clients.

## How to write a blog post: start with the question, not the topic

Most drafts fail before the first sentence is typed, because the writer starts with a subject ("content marketing") instead of a question ("how often should I publish a blog post?"). Pick the exact phrase someone would type into a search bar. Write that phrase down. Answer it directly in your opening two or three sentences, in plain language, with no throat-clearing. If a reader can screenshot your first paragraph and get the full answer, you have done the hardest part correctly.

After that, everything else in the piece exists to support, extend, or prove that opening answer.

## Structure that helps both readers and search engines

A blog post is read in two ways at once: by a person scrolling on a phone, and by a crawler indexing your headings. Structure your post for both.

### Use headers as sign-posts

Each H2 should describe what the section delivers, not just name a topic. "Benefits of exercise" tells a reader nothing. "How much exercise you need per week" tells them exactly what they'll get if they keep reading. Include at least one header phrased as a question, since that is often the literal query a reader searched.

### Keep paragraphs short

Three to four sentences per paragraph is a reasonable ceiling for web reading. Break up long explanations with bullet lists when you're presenting:

- A sequence of steps
- A set of options to compare
- A list of examples or data points

### Put the primary keyword where it matters

Use your main phrase in the title, in the first 100 words, and in at least one header. After that, write for a human. Repeating a phrase past the point of natural language reads as spam to both readers and search algorithms.

> A blog post that only satisfies a search algorithm rarely satisfies a reader, and a post that only satisfies a reader rarely ranks. The best drafts do both at once.

## How long should a blog post be?

There is no fixed number, but a useful range for most informational posts is 900 to 1,500 words. That's long enough to answer a question fully, with examples and sub-points, and short enough that a reader finishes it. Posts competing for complex or technical queries sometimes need 2,000 words or more to cover the topic properly. The test isn't word count, it's whether every paragraph earns its place. Cut any sentence that restates something you already said.

## Where research fits into the process

A claim without a number or a named example is an opinion. A claim with one is evidence. Before you write a section, gather at least one concrete fact to anchor it: a statistic, a study, a specific tool, a real example. This is where sophisticated software and engines that aid us can speed up the search for sources, but a person still checks each fact against its original source before it goes into a draft. That verification step is what separates a post you can stand behind from one that just sounds confident.

## Editing for rhythm and clarity

Once the draft is done, read it aloud. Sentences that are hard to say aloud are usually hard to read. Look for:

- Sentences over 25 words that could split into two
- Repeated words or phrases within the same paragraph
- Adverbs that add length without adding meaning
- Any paragraph that could be deleted without losing information

Cut the sentence, don't soften it. A shorter, plainer sentence almost always beats a longer one dressed up with qualifiers.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to write a good blog post?

For a well-researched 1,000 to 1,500 word post, plan on two to four hours: roughly one hour for research and outlining, one to two hours for drafting, and thirty to sixty minutes for editing.

### Do I need to know SEO to write a blog post?

You need the basics: a clear primary keyword, that keyword in the title and first paragraph, descriptive headers, and a meta description under 160 characters. Beyond that, writing clearly for a human reader does most of the SEO work on its own.

### What's the difference between a blog post and a full article?

In practice, little. "Blog post" usually implies a shorter, more conversational piece tied to a single question, while "article" can imply more depth or reporting. Both should follow the same structure: answer first, evidence throughout, clear headers.

Social media management services typically include strategy and planning, content creation, scheduling and posting, community engagement, and monthly reporting. What varies by agency is the depth of each piece: some firms post on your behalf with a template calendar, others build original content around a research process and adjust it based on what performs. The gap between those two shows up fast once you're three months in.

If you're comparing proposals right now, the line items usually look similar. The work behind them does not.

## What social media management services actually cover

Most agreements bundle five components, though the ratio between them differs by agency size and price point.

- **Strategy and content planning.** A content calendar built around your goals, audience, and platform mix, usually set 4-6 weeks out and revised monthly.
- **Content production.** Writing captions, editing video, designing graphics or carousels, and formatting posts for each platform's specs (aspect ratio, caption length, hashtag conventions).
- **Scheduling and publishing.** Loading content into a tool like Later, Sprout Social, or Buffer, and confirming it posts at the right time for your audience's time zone.
- **Community management.** Responding to comments and direct messages, flagging anything that needs your voice directly, and monitoring mentions.
- **Reporting.** A monthly or biweekly summary of reach, engagement, follower growth, and which posts drove the most action.

Agencies that do this well also run a research step before content gets made: pulling audience questions, checking what's ranking or trending in your niche, and reviewing what your last 90 days of posts actually did. That step is the difference between a calendar that looks busy and one that moves a metric you care about.

## What's usually billed separately

A few things get left out of base packages more often than clients expect:

- **Paid promotion.** Ad spend and campaign management are almost always quoted separately from organic management.
- **Video production beyond editing.** Filming, sound design, and motion graphics for long-form or podcast-derived content is often a separate line, even at agencies that offer it.
- **Influencer or partnership outreach.** Sourcing and negotiating with other creators typically sits outside standard packages.
- **Website or landing page work.** Some agencies will build a link-in-bio page or a simple landing page, but it's rarely included by default.
- **Rebrands or visual identity work.** A new logo, color system, or template set is usually a one-time project fee, not part of monthly retainer.

Ask for a line-item breakdown before you sign. A one-page scope document that names exactly what's covered protects both sides when priorities shift in month three.

## What should social media management services cost?

Pricing depends on platform count, posting frequency, and whether original content (photo, video, design) is produced in-house or client-supplied. As a rough range:

- **Solo freelancer, light management:** $500-$1,500/month, usually 2-3 platforms, 8-12 posts monthly, minimal original content.
- **Small agency, full service:** $2,000-$6,000/month, typically covers strategy, content production, scheduling, and community management across 3-4 platforms.
- **Studio-level, content-led work:** $6,000-$15,000+/month, includes original video, motion graphics, sound design, and a research process behind the calendar, not just execution of one.

> The real cost difference between agencies isn't the retainer number. It's whether the content is built from research on your audience or filled in from a generic template.

If a quote seems low relative to the volume promised, ask who is actually writing captions and editing video, and how many accounts that person manages at once. A social media manager juggling 15 clients cannot give any single one the attention a strategy deck implies.

## How do you evaluate a proposal before signing?

Three questions cut through most sales language:

1. **Who touches the work, and how many clients do they carry?** A named point person managing 4-6 accounts produces different results than a rotating team managing 20.
2. **What does the first 30 days actually produce?** Ask for a sample calendar or draft captions before you commit, not after.
3. **How is success measured, and how often do you see it?** Reach and follower count are the easiest numbers to report and the least tied to business outcomes. Ask what they track instead: saves, shares, click-throughs, inbound inquiries.

## A sample month, broken down

For a full-service retainer across three platforms, a typical month might include:

- Week 1: Audience and trend research, content calendar draft, 2 rounds of client review
- Weeks 2-4: 12-16 pieces of content produced and scheduled, daily community monitoring, one mid-month check-in
- End of month: Performance report with 3-5 specific recommendations for the next cycle

That cadence assumes content is being made, not just repurposed. If an agency is only reformatting your existing assets, the timeline compresses and the price should too.

## FAQ

### Do social media management services include content creation?
Most do, but the depth varies. Some agencies only schedule content you supply; others write captions, edit video, and design graphics as part of the retainer. Confirm which model you're buying.

### How many platforms should one retainer cover?
Most full-service packages cover 3-4 platforms. Adding more without increasing budget usually thins out attention on each one rather than adding value.

### How long before social media management services show results?
Follower and engagement shifts are visible within 30-60 days. Business outcomes like inbound leads or sales typically take 90 days or more, since they depend on consistent posting and an accurate read on what your audience responds to.

Done-for-you content means an outside team handles the full production cycle for your podcast, newsletter, or social presence: research, scripting, filming or writing, editing, and scheduling. You approve the direction and the final cuts. Someone else does the work in between.

The term gets used loosely, so it helps to define the boundary. Done-for-you is different from done-with-you (coaching, templates, feedback on your drafts) and different from a single freelancer who edits your video but leaves research and captions to you. A true done-for-you setup covers the whole chain, from the first outline to the published post.

## What's included in a done-for-you content package

Most done-for-you arrangements bundle a consistent set of deliverables. The exact mix depends on your format, but a standard package usually covers:

- **Research and strategy**: topic selection, keyword or audience research, a content calendar tied to your goals
- **Production support**: podcast or video scripting, interview prep, on-site or remote shoot coordination
- **Post-production**: editing, sound design, motion graphics, captions
- **Repurposing**: short-form clips, quote cards, carousels pulled from long-form material
- **Written assets**: articles, newsletters, show notes
- **Distribution mechanics**: a posting schedule, platform formatting, sometimes community replies

A studio that only offers editing is not done-for-you. A studio that only writes captions from a document you send is not done-for-you either. The defining feature is that raw ideas go in one end and finished, scheduled content comes out the other, with your review built into the middle rather than happening after the fact.

### What you still control

Done-for-you doesn't mean hands-off. You still set the point of view, approve topics before scripting starts, and sign off on drafts before anything publishes. The work that moves off your plate is the execution: the hours of editing, formatting, and calendar management that don't require your specific expertise to complete.

## Who done-for-you content is for

This model fits people whose time is worth more spent elsewhere than in an editing timeline or a scheduling tool. That's usually:

- **Experts and consultants** who have deep material (a book, years of client work, a podcast archive) but no production bandwidth
- **Founders and executives** who need a consistent public presence but can't own the calendar themselves
- **Practitioners with a backlog**: hours of unedited interviews, old talks, or research sitting unused because nobody has time to turn it into posts

> Done-for-you content works when the bottleneck is production, not expertise. If the bottleneck is expertise, no amount of editing help will fix it.

It's a weaker fit for someone still figuring out their point of view or their audience. Strategy work benefits from iteration you do yourself first. Bringing in a full production team before you know what you want to say usually means paying to produce content you'll want to redo in six months.

## How much does done-for-you content cost?

Pricing depends on volume and format, but a few patterns hold across the industry:

- **Clipping and repurposing only** (turning existing long-form into shorts and quote cards) tends to run lower, often priced per piece of source material
- **Full production** (research through scheduling, across podcast, newsletter, and social) is typically a monthly retainer, since it requires ongoing calendar management rather than one-off projects
- **Scope drives cost more than seniority does**: a package covering five platforms costs more than one covering two, regardless of who's doing the editing

Ask any prospective studio for a breakdown by deliverable, not just a flat monthly number. That breakdown tells you what you're actually paying for and makes it easier to compare quotes across vendors.

## What to check before you hire someone

A few questions separate a studio that will actually run your content from one that will just take your money for editing:

- **Who does the research?** If nobody on the team is reading your source material closely, the content will sound generic within a few weeks.
- **What's the review process?** You should see drafts before anything publishes, on a schedule you agree to up front.
- **Is the work human-reviewed at every stage?** Editing and scripting tools speed up production, but a person should be checking tone, accuracy, and fit with your voice before anything goes out.
- **Can they show a workflow, not just a portfolio?** A finished reel tells you the output was good once. A described process tells you whether it'll stay good over twelve months.

## FAQ

### Does done-for-you content include strategy, or just execution?
A proper done-for-you package includes strategy: topic selection, calendar planning, and audience research. Execution-only services (editing or writing without planning) are a narrower, cheaper category.

### How is done-for-you content different from a virtual assistant?
A virtual assistant typically follows instructions you provide. A done-for-you team originates the plan, does the research, and brings you drafts to approve, rather than waiting for detailed direction on each task.

### Can done-for-you content still sound like me?
Yes, if the team studies your existing material closely and builds a style guide from it before producing anything. Voice consistency depends on research depth, not on how much you personally write.

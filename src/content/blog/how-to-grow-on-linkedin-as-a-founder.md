How to grow on LinkedIn comes down to three things done consistently: a clear point of view, a posting cadence you can sustain for at least 90 days, and engagement that happens before and after you hit publish. Founders who grow fast usually post 3-4 times a week, comment daily on 10-15 relevant posts, and track one metric (saves or replies, not likes) to know what's working.

Most advice on this topic is either generic ("be consistent") or gamed ("post at 8am Tuesday"). Neither helps a founder or expert with 45 minutes a week to spend on this. What follows is the system we build with clients, minus the fluff.

## Why most founders plateau at 2,000-5,000 followers

The typical pattern: someone posts for six weeks, gets a few posts to 10,000+ impressions, feels good, then slows down. Growth stalls because the account was optimized for a viral moment instead of a recognizable voice. LinkedIn's algorithm rewards accounts that get consistent early engagement (first 60-90 minutes), which means it rewards a warm network more than clever hooks.

The fix is unglamorous. You need:

- A defined point of view (the 3-5 opinions you're known for, not just your industry)
- A small group of 20-30 people who reliably engage early (peers, past clients, team)
- A format mix so the algorithm and the reader don't get bored

## How to grow on LinkedIn: the weekly system

Here's the cadence that works for founders with limited time, built around four post types per week.

### 1. The point-of-view post (1x/week)
A specific, arguable claim from your work. Not "leadership matters," but something like "most founders under-hire on distribution and over-hire on product past $2M ARR." This is the post that gets saved and shared into DMs.

### 2. The story or lesson post (1x/week)
A real moment from building your company or practice, with a concrete detail (a number, a date, a decision that backfired). Readers trust specificity more than advice.

### 3. The teaching post (1x/week)
A breakdown, framework, or checklist. This is the format that earns saves, which LinkedIn weights heavily in ranking.

### 4. The engagement post or comment sprint (ongoing)
Some weeks this is a question post. Every week it includes 15-20 minutes commenting on other people's posts before you publish your own. This is not optional. Accounts that only broadcast plateau; accounts that also show up in other people's comment sections grow their reach through association.

> The algorithm rewards relationships more than hooks. A post from an account with a warm, active network of 500 will often outperform a post from an account with 20,000 cold followers.

## What actually moves the needle in the first 90 days

Data from creator accounts we've studied (our own and the ones we support) points to three levers that matter more than volume:

- **Dwell time.** Posts that keep readers on the page longer (via line breaks, a story arc, a genuine hook) get pushed further than short takes.
- **Early comments over early likes.** A comment in the first hour signals relevance more than 50 likes with no comments.
- **Format rotation.** Alternating text posts, single-image posts, and short native video keeps your account from looking like a template. Carousels work well for teaching content but should not be more than one in four posts.

What doesn't move the needle much: hashtags (use 2-3 max, they're a minor signal), tagging people who aren't relevant (this hurts reach), and posting more than once a day (this splits your own audience's attention and rarely compounds).

## Do you need to post every day to grow on LinkedIn?

No. Three to four posts a week, done with intention, consistently outperforms daily posting done without a plan. Daily posting only works if you have a team or system supporting research, drafting, and editing, because burnout is the most common reason founder accounts go quiet after 90 days. Sustainable cadence beats maximum output.

## Building a system instead of chasing posts

The founders who grow past 10,000 engaged followers (not just followers, engaged ones) treat LinkedIn like a content operation, not a diary. That means:

- A running list of 30+ post ideas pulled from client calls, sales objections, and industry debates
- A monthly review of which post types earned the most saves and comments
- A repeatable process for turning one idea into three formats (a post, a carousel, a short video script)

## FAQ

### How long does it take to grow on LinkedIn as a founder?
Most accounts see a meaningful shift in reach and inbound interest between 60 and 90 days of consistent posting (3-4x/week) paired with daily engagement. Follower count is a lagging indicator; comments and saves usually rise first.

### What's the ideal posting frequency to grow on LinkedIn?
Three to four posts a week, with 15-20 minutes of commenting on other accounts daily, outperforms daily posting without an engagement routine for most founders and experts.

### Do carousels or video grow faster on LinkedIn?
Both work, but rotation matters more than picking one. Carousels earn saves for teaching content; short native video earns watch time and reach. Relying on only one format usually plateaus within a few months.
